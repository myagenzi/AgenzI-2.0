## 4. Estimates and caps

**4.1** Before a project begins we give you an **estimate** (expected minutes) and a **cap** (maximum minutes).

## 5. The wallet

**5.1** Unused minutes at the end of a billing month move to your wallet.

**5.2 Rollover cap.** Your wallet holds a maximum of **2× your monthly package**. Minutes above this do not accrue.

**5.3 Expiry.** Wallet minutes expire **12 months** from the date they entered the wallet. Oldest minutes are used first.

**5.4 Active subscription required.** The wallet operates alongside an active package. If your subscription ends, wallet minutes are held for **6 months**. Resume within that period and they remain available. After 6 months they expire.

**5.5 Scope.** Wallet minutes may be used for **Standard-rate work only**. Senior and Principal work is drawn from your current month's package.

**5.6 Notice for large drawdowns.** Using more than **1,000 wallet minutes in a calendar month** requires **14 days' written notice** so we can schedule resources.

**5.7 No cash value.** Wallet minutes are not transferable, not refundable, and cannot be exchanged for money or credit.

**5.8 Order of use.** Current month's package first, then wallet minutes, oldest first.

# THINGS THESE DOCUMENTS DELIBERATELY DON'T COVER

Raise these with a lawyer when you review:

**Synthetic media consent agreement.** Clause 11.4 references it. It doesn't exist yet. **Do not sell presenter/avatar work until it does.**

**Data Processing Agreement.** Enterprise and Canadian clients will ask for a separate DPA. Have a template ready before you need it in a negotiation.

**Sub-processor list.** A formal, maintained list of every vendor touching client data. The Privacy Policy table is a start; a proper list is versioned and dated.

**Insurance.** Professional indemnity is referenced nowhere because you don't have it. Some B2B buyers require it before contracting.

**Jurisdiction reality check.** If you're in India and your client is in the US, a clause saying Indian courts have exclusive jurisdiction is normal but may be resisted by larger clients. Know your fallback position before it comes up in a negotiation.
