## THE FOUR TO WRITE

**1. TECH / AI**
Suggested: the most technically credible thing you've built. OCR, an automation, a custom system, an integration. This is the one that proves you're not just a creative shop.

**2. VIDEO**
Suggested: something where the time difference is dramatic and provable. A campaign with regional versions works well — the variant count is its own evidence.

**3. WEBSITE**
Suggested: a build where you can show before and after, and name the timeline.

**4. MARKETING**
Suggested: something with a number attached. Campaign performance, lead volume, response time.

⚠️ **Only claim numbers you can defend.** One case study with a real, modest figure is worth more than four with impressive vague ones. If a client won't let you name them, "a 40-person logistics company in Hyderabad" works fine.

# BUILD NOTES

**Launch state.** If case studies aren't written by 6pm tomorrow, this page ships with the portfolio grid and logos only, and the case study section is **removed entirely** — not left as empty frames. A visitor who sees "Featured Case Studies" above blank cards concludes there aren't any.

**Holding copy if the page is thin:**
> *"We're writing up four projects properly — the situation, the work, and how long it took. Up this month. In the meantime, the free report will show you what we'd do for your business."*

**The "How long it took" table is the most important element on this page.** It's the only place your entire positioning gets proven rather than claimed. Design it to stand out. If a case study can't fill it honestly, that case study isn't ready.

**Fair comparison matters.** "Traditionally 3 weeks" needs a source — an actual quote the client received, their previous vendor's timeline, or an internal estimate. State which. An unsourced comparison is the kind of thing a sceptical buyer will challenge, and you only need to be caught once.

**Client permission.** Before naming any client, logo or quote, get written permission. Email is fine. This matters more than it seems — using a logo without consent is a real dispute and an avoidable one.

**Anonymised is fine.** "A 40-person logistics company in Hyderabad" carries almost the same weight as a name, and it removes the permission bottleneck entirely.

**Filter bar is optional for V1.** With four case studies and twelve portfolio cards it's not doing much work. Add it when the grid grows past twenty.
